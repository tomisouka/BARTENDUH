Section 02 — The Foundation

🍺 Ales vs Lagers — The Two Families

All beer on earth falls into one of two categories based on yeast type and fermentation temperature. Everything else — flavor, color, bitterness — flows from this fundamental split.
🔴 ALES
YeastTop-fermenting
TemperatureWarmer (60–75°F)
FermentationFaster (days–weeks)
FlavorComplex, fruity, robust
AgeOldest beer style
ExamplesIPA, Stout, Pale Ale, Porter, Hefeweizen, Belgian
🔵 LAGERS
YeastBottom-fermenting
TemperatureCooler (45–55°F)
FermentationSlower (weeks–months)
FlavorClean, crisp, smooth
AgeDeveloped ~1800s Bavaria
ExamplesPilsner, Helles, Bock, Dunkel, Light Lager
Section 03 — Ales

🔴 Ale Styles

Ale · India Pale Ale
IPA
ABV5–7% (Session) · 7–10% (Standard) · 10%+ (Imperial)
ColorGold to amber
TasteIntensely hoppy, bitter, citrus/tropical fruit
BodyMedium to full
The craft beer revolution's flagship style. West Coast = pine, grapefruit, dry, bitter. Hazy/NEIPA = juicy, tropical, smooth, less bitter. Session = lower ABV but still hoppy. Double/Imperial = intense everything.
Dogfish Head 60 Min Sierra Nevada Torpedo Lagunitas IPA Stone IPA Bell's Two Hearted Voodoo Ranger Hazy Little Thing Pliny the Elder
Pairs with: Spicy foods, burgers, sharp cheddar, fried food
Ale · Pale Ale
Pale Ale
ABV4.5–6%
ColorGolden to copper
TasteBalanced malt sweetness + hop bitterness
BodyMedium
Less intense than IPA but more character than a lager. The perfect middle ground. American Pale Ale = citrusy, crisp. English Pale Ale = more malt-forward, earthy hops. Sierra Nevada Pale Ale is the gold standard.
Sierra Nevada Pale Ale Dale's Pale Ale New Belgium Fat Tire Founders Pale Ale Bass Ale Fuller's London Pride
Pairs with: Pizza, roast chicken, salmon, cheddar
Ale · Blonde / Golden Ale
Blonde Ale
ABV4–6%
ColorLight gold to pale amber
TasteLight, crisp, slightly sweet, minimal bitterness
BodyLight to medium
The great gateway ale. Designed to be approachable for lager drinkers. Low bitterness, clean malt, very refreshing. Kölsch is the German version — pale, clean, slightly fruity.
Kona Big Wave Firestone Walker 805 Karbach Love Street Alaskan Summer Reissdorf Kölsch
Pairs with: Salads, seafood, light pasta, mild cheeses
Ale · Brown Ale
Brown Ale
ABV4–6%
ColorAmber to dark brown
TasteToasty, nutty, caramel, chocolate notes
BodyMedium
The underrated middle child of dark beers. More approachable than a stout, more character than a pale ale. Nutty, toasty, slightly sweet. Newcastle is the classic crowd-pleaser.
Newcastle Brown Ale Samuel Smith's Nut Brown Brooklyn Brown Ale Bell's Best Brown Cigar City Maduro
Pairs with: Roasted meats, BBQ, sausages, nutty cheeses
Ale · Porter
Porter
ABV4–6%
ColorDark brown to black
TasteRoasted malt, coffee, chocolate, caramel, toffee
BodyMedium to full
The 18th century London working-class beer. Roasty and rich but smoother and less intense than stout. Coffee, dark chocolate, slight sweetness. Deschutes Black Butte is a benchmark American porter.
Founders Porter Deschutes Black Butte Anchor Porter Great Lakes Edmund Fitzgerald Samuel Smith's Taddy
Pairs with: BBQ, smoked meats, chocolate desserts, blue cheese
Ale · Stout
Stout
ABV4–7% (Dry) · 8–12% (Imperial)
ColorBlack
TasteRich, roasted barley, coffee, dark chocolate
BodyFull, creamy
Dry Stout (Guinness) = coffee-like, lighter body, 4.2% ABV. Milk Stout = lactose sweetness, creamy. Oatmeal Stout = silky smooth. Imperial/Russian Imperial = 8–12%+ ABV, rich and warming. One of the most diverse beer families.
Guinness Draught Left Hand Milk Stout Founders Breakfast Stout Old Rasputin Imperial Samuel Smith's Oatmeal Murphy's Irish Stout
Pairs with: Oysters, beef stew, chocolate cake, vanilla ice cream
Ale · Wheat Beer
Wheat Ale
ABV4–6%
ColorPale to golden, often cloudy
TasteLight, fruity (banana, clove), citrusy, refreshing
BodyLight to medium
Hefeweizen = German style with banana + clove from yeast. Witbier/Belgian White = spiced with coriander and orange peel (Blue Moon). American Wheat = crisper, more citrusy. Unfiltered = naturally cloudy, smooth.
Blue Moon Belgian White Franziskaner Hefeweizen Paulaner Hefeweizen Allagash White Erdinger Bell's Oberon
Pairs with: Seafood, salads, light cheeses, citrus dishes
Ale · Belgian Style
Belgian Ale
ABV6–12%
ColorVaries — pale gold to dark brown
TasteFruity, spicy (clove, pepper), sweet, complex
BodyMedium to full
Dubbel: dark, malty, raisin, 6–8% ABV. Tripel: strong golden, spicy, fruity, 8–10%. Quad: very strong, dark fruit, warming, 9–12%+. Saison: farmhouse ale, peppery, dry, 5–7%. Trappist monastery ales are legendary and world-class.
Chimay Red/White/Blue Duvel Westmalle Tripel Rochefort 10 Delirium Tremens Saison Dupont Westvleteren 12
Pairs with: Rich meats, game, strong cheeses, mussels
Section 04 — Lagers

🔵 Lager Styles

Lager · Pilsner
Pilsner
ABV4–5.5%
ColorPale gold to light amber
TasteCrisp, clean, hoppy, slightly bitter, dry finish
BodyLight to medium
Born in Plzeň, Czech Republic in 1842. Czech Pilsner (Pilsner Urquell) = fuller, more bitter, richer gold. German Pils (Bitburger, Warsteiner) = lighter, crisper, more delicate. The most widely imitated style in history.
Pilsner Urquell Beck's Stella Artois Bitburger Warsteiner Spaten Pils Budweiser Budvar
Pairs with: Fried foods, seafood, salads, light pasta
Lager · Pale / American Lager
Pale Lager
ABV4–5%
ColorVery pale straw to gold
TasteLight, clean, mild, low hop flavor, refreshing
BodyLight
The world's most popular beer style. American lagers (Budweiser, Coors) use corn/rice adjuncts for extra lightness. Mexican lagers (Corona, Modelo) add lime culture. International lagers (Heineken, Asahi) are pilsner-derived. Easy to drink, widely available.
Budweiser Coors Banquet Miller High Life Corona Extra Modelo Especial Heineken Asahi Super Dry Yuengling Lager
Pairs with: BBQ, burgers, pizza, tacos, anything casual
Lager · Light Lager
Light Lager
ABV3–4.2%
ColorVery pale, nearly clear
TasteExtremely light, minimal flavor, low calories
BodyVery light
America's #1 category. Calories: Michelob Ultra (96), Miller Lite (96), Coors Light (102), Bud Light (110). Designed for session drinking. Less flavor by design — maximum refreshment, minimum consequence.
Michelob Ultra (96 cal) Miller Lite (96 cal) Coors Light (102 cal) Bud Light (110 cal) Busch Light Natural Light Keystone Light
Pairs with: Light foods, salads, grilled chicken, fish tacos
Lager · Amber / Vienna Lager
Amber Lager
ABV4.5–5.5%
ColorAmber to copper
TasteMalty, slightly sweet, toasted bread, caramel
BodyMedium
Vienna lager style originated in 19th century Austria. Sam Adams Boston Lager essentially introduced Americans to malty lagers. Negra Modelo is the best-selling dark Mexican beer. Great balance of malt and hop.
Samuel Adams Boston Lager Negra Modelo Brooklyn Lager Dos Equis Amber Yuengling Lager Abita Amber
Pairs with: Roasted chicken, pork, sausages, caramel desserts
Lager · Dark Lager / Dunkel
Dark Lager
ABV4.5–6%
ColorDark brown
TasteMalty, bread-like, chocolate, caramel, smooth
BodyMedium to full
Germany's dark lager tradition predates pale lagers. Dunkel means "dark" in German. Munich's answer to stout — rich malt flavor without heavy roast. Schwarzbier (black beer) is an even darker variant. Smooth and approachable.
Köstritzer Schwarzbier Paulaner Dunkel Hofbräu Dunkel Shiner Bock Sam Adams Black Lager
Pairs with: Roasted meats, sausages, bread, pretzels
Lager · Bock
Bock
ABV6–7.5% (Traditional) · 7–10% (Doppelbock) · 9–14% (Eisbock)
ColorAmber to dark brown
TasteMalty, sweet, full-bodied, caramel, toffee
BodyFull
Bavaria's strong lager tradition. Traditional Bock (6–7%): Shiner Bock is America's most popular. Maibock: lighter, spring seasonal. Doppelbock (7–10%): richer, darker — Paulaner Salvator is the archetype. Eisbock: freeze-concentrated to reach 9–14%, incredibly powerful.
Shiner Bock Paulaner Salvator Ayinger Celebrator Spaten Optimator Schneider Aventinus Eisbock Sam Adams Double Bock
Pairs with: Roasted pork, sausages, hearty stews, aged cheeses

Section 06 — Decision Guide

🎯 How to Choose Your Beer

Want something light & refreshing?
→
Pilsner, Pale Lager, Light Lager, Wheat Ale, Blonde Ale
Want something hoppy & bitter?
→
IPA (West Coast for dry/bitter, Hazy for smooth/juicy), Pale Ale
Want something dark & rich?
→
Stout, Porter, Dark Lager, Bock
Want something balanced?
→
Pale Ale, Amber Lager, Brown Ale
Want something fruity & complex?
→
Belgian Ale, Hefeweizen, Saison
Want low calories?
→
Light Lager (Michelob Ultra, Bud Light, Miller Lite) or Hard Seltzer
Want high alcohol?
→
Imperial IPA, Imperial Stout, Belgian Tripel/Quad, Doppelbock
New to craft beer?
→
Blonde Ale, Hefeweizen, Pale Ale — eases you in gently
I only drink lagers, want to try something new?
→
Blonde Ale or Pale Ale — closest ale to a lager in drinkability
Section 07 — Food Pairing

🍽️ Beer & Food Pairing

Pairing Principles: Match intensity (light food = light beer, rich food = rich beer). Complement (roasted meat + roasted stout). Contrast (spicy food + refreshing pilsner). Cut through fat (hoppy IPA cuts grease). Carbonation cleanses the palate between bites.

Section 10 — The Great Debate

🏅 Craft Beer vs Macro Beer

🏭 Macro Beer (Big Brands)

Budweiser, Coors, Miller, Heineken, Corona

  • Mass-produced; highly consistent batch to batch
  • Lighter, more accessible flavors
  • Lower cost — best value per ounce
  • Available everywhere, any hour
  • Built for easy session drinking
  • Global recognition and cultural presence
🍻 Craft Beer (Independent)

Sierra Nevada, Dogfish Head, Stone, local breweries

  • Small-batch, experimental, creative
  • Bold, complex, diverse flavors
  • Higher cost — artisanal production
  • Regional or limited availability
  • Wide style variety — hundreds of sub-styles
  • Seasonal and one-off releases
Both have their place. Macro for easy drinking and social occasions. Craft for exploration, tasting, and finding your personal favorites. The best beer is the one you enjoy.
Section 11 — Around the World

🌍 Regional Beer Cultures

🇺🇸
American Beer Culture
  • Light lagers dominate the volume market (Bud Light, Coors Light)
  • Craft beer boom since the 1980s — IPAs are now king
  • West Coast = hoppy, bitter IPAs; East Coast = hazy, juicy IPAs
  • Growing seltzer, cider, and non-alcoholic markets
🇩🇪
German Beer Culture
  • Reinheitsgebot (Purity Law) since 1516: only water, malt, hops, yeast
  • Pilsners, Hefeweizens, Bocks, Dunkel — all regional specialties
  • Beer gardens are a cultural institution, especially in Bavaria
  • Oktoberfest: 6+ million liters of Märzen served annually in Munich
🇧🇪
Belgian Beer Culture
  • UNESCO recognized Belgian beer culture in 2016
  • Trappist monasteries brew some of the world's most sought-after ales
  • 800+ distinct beers in a country smaller than Maryland
  • Sour ales (lambic, gueuze) use wild fermentation — unique worldwide
🇬🇧
British Beer Culture
  • Cask ale (real ale) is a uniquely British tradition
  • CAMRA (Campaign for Real Ale) has protected traditional brewing since 1971
  • Porters, stouts, and pale ales are the backbone
  • Served warmer than American standards; lower carbonation
🇲🇽
Mexican Beer Culture
  • Light lagers served ice cold with lime and salt
  • Beach and casual outdoor drinking culture
  • Corona, Modelo, Pacifico dominate domestic and export markets
  • Michelada: beer + lime juice + hot sauce + salt rim — a national tradition
🇨🇿
Czech Beer Culture
  • World's highest per capita beer consumption
  • Birthplace of the modern lager (Pilsner, 1842)
  • Beer is often cheaper than water in Prague restaurants
  • Unpasteurized "tank beer" (tankové pivo) is a Czech specialty
Section 12 — The Language of Beer

📚 Beer Terminology

ABV
Alcohol By Volume — the % of alcohol in the beer. Standard beer is 4–6%. Imperial styles can reach 12–15%.
IBU
International Bitterness Units — measures hop bitterness. Light lagers: 5–15 IBU. Double IPAs: 70–100+ IBU.
Session Beer
Lower ABV beer (under 5%) designed for drinking over an extended period without heavy intoxication.
Hazy / Unfiltered
Cloudy appearance from suspended yeast and proteins. Associated with New England IPAs — smooth, soft mouthfeel.
Dry-Hopped
Hops added during or after fermentation rather than the boil. Adds intense aroma without bitterness.
Draft / Draught
Beer served from a pressurized keg through a tap system. Generally fresher than bottled or canned.
Nitro
Nitrogenated beer — uses nitrogen instead of CO₂. Creates tiny bubbles, creamy texture, smooth head. Guinness is the famous example.
Bomber
A 22 oz (650ml) bottle — common for craft beer releases and limited editions.
Growler
A 64 oz (half-gallon) refillable bottle used to take draft beer home from a brewery or taproom.
Cask
Unpasteurized, naturally carbonated beer served at cellar temperature. The traditional "real ale" format in Britain.
Reinheitsgebot
German Beer Purity Law of 1516: only water, malted barley, hops, and yeast permitted. Still followed by many German brewers.
Adjuncts
Non-malt fermentables added to beer — corn, rice (used in American lagers), oats, wheat, lactose, fruit.
Lagering
Cold-conditioning process where lagers rest at near-freezing temps for weeks/months to develop clean, crisp flavor.
Trappist
Beer brewed by or under supervision of Trappist monks. Only 14 breweries worldwide qualify. Highly sought-after.
Skunked
Off-flavor caused by UV light reacting with hop compounds. Clear and green bottles are most vulnerable. Brown bottles protect best.
Malt
Barley that's been germinated and dried. The main source of fermentable sugars and the foundation of beer's body, color, and sweetness.
