Old World & New World

🌍 Wine Regions by Country

Old World = Europe (France, Italy, Spain, Germany). Typically more restrained, earthy, mineral. New World = USA, Australia, Chile, Argentina. Typically riper, fruitier, more straightforward labeling by grape name.
🇫🇷
France
Old World · The Gold Standard
  • Bordeaux — Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot blends
  • Burgundy — Pinot Noir, Chardonnay
  • Champagne — Sparkling wines
  • Rhône Valley — Syrah, Grenache, Viognier
  • Alsace — Riesling, Gewürztraminer
  • Loire Valley — Sauvignon Blanc (Sancerre), Chenin Blanc
  • Provence — Rosé wines
🇮🇹
Italy
Old World · 350+ Native Grapes
  • Tuscany — Chianti, Brunello, Super Tuscans
  • Piedmont — Barolo, Barbaresco (Nebbiolo)
  • Veneto — Pinot Grigio, Amarone, Prosecco
  • Sicily — Nero d'Avola, Marsala
  • Friuli — Pinot Grigio, Friulano
🇪🇸
Spain
Old World · Rioja & Beyond
  • Rioja — Tempranillo; Crianza, Reserva, Gran Reserva
  • Ribera del Duero — Tempranillo (bold, concentrated)
  • Priorat — Garnacha, Cariñena (powerful reds)
  • Rías Baixas — Albariño (crisp whites)
  • Cava — Sparkling wines (Catalonia)
  • Jerez — Sherry (fortified)
🇩🇪
Germany
Old World · White Wine Paradise
  • Mosel — Riesling (slate soils, delicate)
  • Rheingau — Riesling (fuller, richer)
  • Pfalz — Riesling, Spätburgunder (Pinot Noir)
  • Baden — Pinot Noir (warmest German region)

🍽️ Wine & Food Pairing

The Golden Rules: Match weight (light food = light wine). Contrast or complement flavors. High-acid wines cut through fat. Sweet wines balance spicy food. Regional pairings almost always work.

Glassware Guide
  • Bordeaux glass — Tall, large bowl; Cab Sauv, Merlot
  • Burgundy glass — Wide bowl; Pinot Noir, delicate reds
  • Universal glass — Medium bowl; everyday wines
  • White wine glass — Narrower bowl; whites, rosé
  • Flute — Tall, narrow; Champagne, Prosecco, Cava
  • Coupe — Wide, shallow; cocktails, fun sparkling
  • Port glass — Small; fortified & dessert wines
🏠
Storing Wine
  • Ideal temp: 55°F / 13°C (consistent)
  • Store bottles on their side (keeps cork moist)
  • Avoid sunlight, heat, and vibration
  • Humidity: 60–70% ideal
  • Most wines are meant to drink within 1–3 years
  • Fine wine (Barolo, Burgundy, Bordeaux) can age 10–50+ years
  • Once opened: reseal, refrigerate, drink within 3–5 days
👃
Tasting Wine Like a Pro
  • 1. Look — Color, clarity, depth
  • 2. Swirl — Release aromas; note viscosity (legs)
  • 3. Smell — Fruits, earth, oak, flowers, minerality
  • 4. Taste — Sweetness, acidity, tannins, alcohol, body
  • 5. Finish — How long does the flavor linger? (quality indicator)
  • Decant big reds 30–60 min before serving

The Language of Wine

📚 Wine Terminology

Terroir
The complete natural environment: soil, climate, topography. Why the same grape tastes different in different places.
Tannins
Natural compounds from grape skins and oak that create a drying, grippy sensation. Higher in red wines.
Acidity
Makes wine taste bright and crisp. Essential for balance and food-pairing. High acidity = longer aging potential.
Finish / Length
How long the flavor lingers after swallowing. A long finish (30+ seconds) indicates a high-quality wine.
Decanting
Pouring wine into a carafe to expose it to oxygen. Softens tannins and opens up aromas.
Vintage
The year the grapes were harvested. Weather affects quality significantly year-to-year.
Sulfites
Preservatives used in winemaking. "Contains sulfites" is required on labels in many countries. Not the cause of most headaches.
Malolactic Fermentation
Converts sharp malic acid to softer lactic acid. Gives Chardonnay that creamy, buttery texture.
Oxidation
Exposure to air that changes wine's flavor. Good when controlled (decanting); bad when wine goes off.
Noble Rot (Botrytis)
A beneficial fungus that dehydrates grapes, concentrating sugars for sweet wines like Sauternes.
Sur Lie
Aging wine on dead yeast cells. Adds creaminess and complexity. Common in Champagne and Muscadet.
Legs / Tears
Droplets that form on the glass after swirling. Indicate alcohol and sugar content — not quality.
Blind Tasting
Evaluating wine without knowing what it is. Used to remove bias from the judgment process.
Cuvée
A blend or special selection of wine. Especially common in Champagne and Rhône blends.
Natural Wine
Made with minimal intervention: organic grapes, native yeasts, no added sulfites. Often cloudy, funky, alive.
Orange Wine
White wine made like red wine — with extended skin contact. Amber-colored, tannic, complex.
