The Craft

Technique Reference

The Five Fundamentals

Shake
Use when: citrus, juice, cream, egg whites, syrups are involved

Fill shaker ¾ with ice, seal tight, shake hard 10–15 seconds. Listen — the sound changes from "crunchy" to "wet" when done.

Margarita · Daiquiri · Whiskey Sour · Cosmo
Stir
Use when: spirit-only cocktails — no juice, cream, or citrus

Add ice to mixing glass, pour spirits, stir slowly 30–40 seconds. Stir until glass exterior frosts over.

Martini · Manhattan · Negroni · Old Fashioned
Build
Use for: simple highballs and two-ingredient drinks

Ice in glass → spirit → mixer → quick stir. Simple and fast.

G&T · Rum & Coke · Moscow Mule
Muddle
Use for: extracting oils and flavor from herbs or fruit

Herbs: gentle pressure, twist, release oils — don't shred. Fruit: more aggressive to break down.

Mojito · Mint Julep · Old Fashioned (sugar method)
Blend
Use for: frozen drinks

Spirits + mixers + ice, blend until smooth. Use crushed ice if available.

Frozen Margarita · Piña Colada

The Law

The Golden Rules

  1. Fresh citrus juice, always — No bottled juice. Ever.
  2. Measure your ingredients — Use a jigger until you're a pro.
  3. Chill your glassware — Freezer or ice water while mixing.
  4. Stir stirred, shake shaken — Spirit-only = stir; juice/citrus = shake.
  5. Taste as you go — Adjust sweetness, tartness, strength.
  6. Simple garnishes — Must add aroma, flavor, or visual appeal.
  7. Make classics yours — Learn the rules, then adapt to taste.
The Details

Pro Tips

Ice Matters

Standard cubes — most drinks
Large cubes (2") — rocks drinks, slower melt
Crushed ice — Juleps, Tiki drinks
Always use fresh ice. Never reuse.

Citrus Rules

Fresh juice lasts ~4 hours before oxidizing. Lemon for whiskey drinks, lime for rum & tequila.

Vermouth

It's wine. Refrigerate after opening. Use within 1–2 months. Old vermouth = ruined drink.

Garnishes

Twist — Express oils over drink, rub rim, drop or discard
Rim — Citrus wedge + salt/sugar — rim half only to give guest the choice
