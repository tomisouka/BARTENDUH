The Foundation

Grains: The Foundation

Barley
Beer · Scotch · Irish Whiskey
Nutty, bready, slightly sweet. Contains enzymes that convert its own starches to sugar — making it the most efficient brewing grain.
60–65% starch content
Corn
Bourbon · Tennessee Whiskey · Many Vodkas
Sweet, mild, vanilla notes. Highest starch content of any grain — cheap, abundant in America, produces smooth and sweet spirits.
70–75% starch content
Rye
Rye Whiskey · Some Bourbons · Some Vodkas
Spicy, peppery, dry, bold. Adds complexity and bite to whiskey. Creates a thick, sticky mash that's difficult to work with.
55–60% starch content
Wheat
Wheat Beer · Wheated Bourbon · Some Vodkas
Soft, smooth, slightly sweet, light. Creates gentle and approachable spirits — the character of Maker's Mark bourbon.
60–65% starch content

Grain Flavor Contributions

GrainWhat it brings
CornSweetness, vanilla, mild flavor — bourbon's smooth foundation
RyeSpicy bite, peppery notes, dryness — backbone of rye whiskey
WheatSoft, gentle, smooth — makes "wheated" bourbon like Maker's Mark
BarleyNutty, malty, bready — foundation of Scotch and beer
Malted BarleySweeter, richer than raw barley — provides enzymes for ALL other grains
Why bourbon is mostly corn: It's cheap, abundant in America, and produces a sweet, smooth whiskey that ages well in charred oak.

Why Scotch is all barley: Tradition, terroir, and barley grows well in Scotland's cool climate.
The Science

Fermentation: Where the Magic Happens

Yeast (single-celled fungus) metabolizes sugar without oxygen, producing ethanol, carbon dioxide, and heat as byproducts.
C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2 C₂H₅OH + 2 CO₂

The Yeast

Ale Yeast
Saccharomyces cerevisiae
Temp: 60–75°F (warm) · Top-fermenting
Flavor: Fruity esters — banana, apple, pear
Used in: Ales, wine, whiskey, sake
Personality: Fast, aggressive, flavorful
Lager Yeast
Saccharomyces pastorianus
Temp: 45–55°F (cold) · Bottom-fermenting
Flavor: Clean, crisp, very subtle
Used in: Lagers, pilsners
Personality: Slow, patient, neutral
Wild Yeast & Bacteria
Brettanomyces · Lactobacillus · Pediococcus
Brett: Funky, earthy, farmhouse — leather, barnyard, tropical
Lacto: Sour, tart (same as yogurt bacteria)
Used in: Sour beers, natural wine, farmhouse ales

The Four Phases

Phase 1 · 0–24 hours
Lag — Yeast Waking Up
Nothing visible yet. Yeast absorbs oxygen, builds cell walls, multiplies, acclimates to temperature. Critical to get conditions right here.
Phase 2 · 1–4 days
Exponential Growth — The Main Event
Heavy bubbling, thick foam (krausen), temperature rising. Alcohol, CO₂, and flavor compounds (esters, phenols) are actively being produced. Temperature control here makes or breaks the final flavor.
Phase 3 · 3–7 days
Stationary — Cleaning Up
Bubbling slows, foam collapses, liquid starts clearing. Final sugars convert, yeast reabsorbs off-flavors, yeast settles to the bottom.
Phase 4 · Days to Years
Conditioning — Dormant & Done
Clear liquid, sediment layer, no activity. Beer: 1–4 weeks. Spirits: ready to distill. Wine: months to years as complexity develops.

The Golden Rules of Storage

  1. High proof = low risk Spirits last almost forever. Bacteria and mold cannot survive at 40%+ ABV.
  2. Wine needs care Cool, dark, horizontal when unopened. Refrigerate immediately once opened.
  3. Vermouth is wine Refrigerate after opening. Most people forget this — old vermouth ruins cocktails.
  4. Beer is perishable Drink fresh, especially IPAs. Hops fade fast, carbonation escapes, oxidation sets in quickly.
  5. Air is the enemy Less air in the bottle means slower degradation. Transfer to smaller bottles when low.
  6. Heat kills flavor Never store above 75°F for extended periods. Even spirits will lose nuance.
  7. When in doubt, smell it Your nose is your best quality indicator. Vinegar, cardboard, or mustiness? Pour it out.
